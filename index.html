What is this?

In 2017 Topos asked what New York's five boroughs would look like if they were drawn from data instead of 1898 politics. They described each neighborhood as a 65-dimensional vector, reduced it with PCA to 16 dimensions (86% of variance), and ran k-means with K=5. The result was five concentric “boroughs” — Minhattan, The Ring, North Bend, South Bend, The Meadows — that ignored the rivers entirely.

This app recreates that method for many cities using only OpenStreetMap data, and lets you turn the knobs.

Method

  1. Neighborhoods. The city's administrative boundary is tiled with a hexagonal grid (~700–900 cells per city; the radius adapts to city size). Topos used ZIP codes; hexes are consistent across countries.
  2. Vectors. For each hex we count OSM points of interest (bars, cafés, museums, shops, schools, stations, bus stops, street segments, buildings, tall buildings, trees…) and convert them to densities per km² of land, plus land-use fractions (residential, commercial, industrial, green, water, beach). ~50 dimensions.
  3. PCA. Features are (optionally log-) transformed, z-scored, and reduced to the smallest number of principal components explaining the target variance (86% by default).
  4. k-means. Cells are clustered in the reduced space with k-means++ (12 restarts). Clusters are numbered by their average distance to the city centre, so borough 1 is always the most central.

Everything after the OSM extraction runs in your browser, so changing K, the feature groups, or the variance target re-clusters instantly. Cluster names are auto-generated from the most distinctive features; click a name to rename it.

Caveats

OSM coverage varies by city and by feature (e.g. trees and building heights are mapped very unevenly). Clusters describe the mapped city. Topos' original inputs also included image recognition, 311 complaints and other proprietary signals that have no OSM analogue.

Source code and the extraction pipeline are on GitHub. Data extracted with the Overpass API; boundaries from Nominatim.
